The order is target, audit, gift, and only then pitch. Most of the work happens before you write anything. If you can describe their problem better than they can, they'll assume you can fix it.

Before writing: answer six questions per target (5 minutes)

QuestionWhat to write down
Who are they?Their niche, and what they fear in their own words. Get it from their videos and comments, e.g. "I post every week and nobody new finds me."
Why you?Facts that are true of you that nobody else says. You have two. First, your channel had 5 subscribers. Your second Short got 1,500 views with only background music added, and 7–8 Shorts took you from 5 to 15 subscribers. That shows the views come from the cut and the hook, not from an existing audience. Second, you broke down 511 of Mark Manson's Shorts, and the ones that open on a named idea broke out more than twice as often.
What do they get?Keep asking "so what?" until you reach the outcome. 10 Shorts → their best moments get seen → strangers find the long videos → new clients without extra filming.
What can you point at?Only things you can show, never adjectives: the sample Short, a timestamp from their video, a peer's Shorts views, and your own channel's Studio numbers (5 → 15 subs, 1,500 views on your second Short). Keep a screenshot ready for anyone who asks.
Why now?The cost of waiting: every episode without Shorts is reach they won't get back. Also real scarcity, since your 28-hour cap means about 5 packs a week.
How does it work?Most creators think Shorts means filming more. Wrong, because the Shorts are already in the episode, buried at minute 20. You cut them out on the strongest line.

Audit (10 minutes)

  • When was their last Short? How many in the last 30 days?
  • Find the one moment with the best hook: a big number, a turnaround story, or a question their audience argues about.
  • Find a peer in their niche who posts Shorts, and note one real view count.
  • Find the pain in their own words, from a video, their About page or a comment reply.
  • Note what they sell (course, coaching, sponsor), so you know what they get from more reach.

Gift, then send

  • Cut 1 Short: the moment most likely to stop a scroll. Open on its strongest line.
  • Upload them unlisted. Links, never attachments.
  • Subject line: what they get, from their own video, with no effort on their part.
  • Read it out loud and cut anything you stumble on. Paste it into Hemingway (hemingwayapp.com) and aim for grade 6 or lower.
  • Under 130 words, one ask, and vary your sentence length.
